Teaching

Teaching statement

My teaching philosophy has always been based upon the premise that all people, at whatever stage in their education, are students with the desire and capacity for intellectual growth. That is, I am not, as a teacher, a mere source of information. I am, rather, a person whose own education thus far has provided me with the experience to help formulate the questions which lead to a more profound understanding not only of science or law, but of nature in general and the history of ideas.

My classroom is above all a forum for a never-ending dialog and a communal exploration of scientific and humanistic propositions. In the lecture hall, however many students there may be (from a small prison class of 10–15 to a hall of 100 law students), it is still vitally important that the students see my presentations not as reiterations of conclusions and dogmas, but as demonstrations of discussions in which they, the students, are equally involved.
