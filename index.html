David Michael Nolta Garavito, J.D., Ph.D.

Judson Falknor Professor of Law, University of Washington School of Law

I study judgment and decision making in legal and public health contexts, drawing on criminal law and procedure, developmental cognition, and neuroscience.

I am the Judson Falknor Professor of Law at the University of Washington School of Law. I received my M.A. and Ph.D. in Human Development from Cornell University as part of Valerie Reyna’s Laboratory for Rational Decision Making, and a J.D. from Cornell Law School. I completed my post-doctoral training as an Interprofessional Polytrauma & Traumatic Brain Injury Rehabilitation Research Fellow for the Department of Veterans Affairs under the supervision of Jim Bjork.

Research

My research examines the perception of risks and decision making within various contexts (parole outcomes, sports, substance use, vaccination, etc.) and the cognitive and neural effects of concussive and sub-concussive injuries. I also conduct research on memory across the lifespan to evaluate susceptibility to semantic false memories, and I am using advanced theory-based models of human memory to detect the development and progression of possible neurodegenerative diseases in at-risk populations.

Law & policy

As a graduate of Cornell Law School and a New York attorney, my legal experience has provided me with the insight to write on legal and policy matters. My interests relate to criminal law and procedure, human development, race and socioeconomic status, and the application of psychology and neuroscience. I have published work in the Cornell Journal of Law & Public Policy and the American Criminal Law Review on such topics, and I am currently writing several manuscripts on contemporary issues in law and technology, specifically as they relate to privacy or human development.

Software

I develop Python-based programs, the best example of which is TaskMaster, a free and open-source application that runs many common behavioral research tasks on Mac, Windows, and Linux computers.
